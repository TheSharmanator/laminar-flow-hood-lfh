LFH v1 Build Manual

A fully printed, Gordotek-style hood: HEPA filter on the front, 150 mm inline fan blowing down into a sealed box, with an intake bell and pre-filter on top, and a detachable clear visor on 6 × 3 mm magnets. Built to the measured parts: a 343 × 295 HEPA and a 148 mm fan. Every check in the reference model passes at these sizes.

PETG notes

Expect frosted-clear rather than glass, usually a touch clearer than clear PLA. The visor clearances were proven in PLA and PETG prints a hair fat, so ease a snug V1/V2 knob joint with a file. Thin superglue grips PETG less reliably: scuff the faces first, or bed the magnets in 2-part epoxy.

Print order

P00 + P00b first. Then the box quarters (longest prints, about 227 mm tall), then everything else in any order.

6 · Assembly

18 steps, each with no more than two actions and one check. Don't move on until the check passes. Everything you need is in section 1.

Box

Flange-down on a flat surface (glass, worktop, the printer plate). Every piece drops in from above; the surface is the end stop.

  1. 01

    Start with Q1 and Q2

    • Stand Q2 flange-down on the flat surface.
    • Lower Q1 in from above so its dovetail drops into Q2's slot.

    CheckBoth flanges sit flat. No rock.

  2. 02

    Add Q3

    • Lower Q3 alongside Q2 the same way, sliding its slot down over Q2's tongue.

    CheckThe tongue disappears into the slot all the way to the surface.

  3. 03

    Q4 goes in last

    • Lower Q4 into the gap. It engages two seams at once.

    CheckAll four flanges flat, seams closed. If Q4 refuses, something is in the wrong spot. That is the coding doing its job.

  4. 04

    Glue the box

    • Run thin superglue along every seam, inside and out, including the four flange seams.
    • Leave it 10 minutes without moving it.

    CheckRap the walls. Nothing creaks or shifts.

Back

Same flat surface. The back goes on rear-edge down into a silicone-filled channel.

  1. 05

    Assemble the back plate

    • Lay B1–B4 ridges-up and press the jigsaw tabs together.
    • Wick thin superglue along the cross seam.

    CheckPlate flat. The U-channel runs unbroken all round.

  2. 06

    Fit the back

    • Run a continuous bead of neutral-cure silicone along the bottom of the U-channel.
    • Lower the box in, rear edge first, and weight it.

    CheckSilicone squeezes out on both sides all round. Leave 24 h.

Top and seal

Box upright from here on.

  1. 07

    Fit the fan socket

    • Glue the socket flange inside the engraved ring on the top (gland notch, if there is one, to the back).
    • Run a silicone bead round the flange edge.

    CheckBead fully closed. No gaps at the top seam.

  2. 08

    Seal the inside

    • Mask the flange front face, where the gasket goes.
    • Stir the tin, then brush 2–3 thin coats of Plasti Dip inside, through the front opening. Give each coat about 30 minutes.

    CheckTorch inside: no bare seam lines. Peel the masking before it fully cures.

Clamp, nuts, gasket

The filter seal is the only seal that really matters. Unfiltered air leaking here goes straight onto your work.

  1. 09

    Build the clamp frame

    • Front-face-down on the flat surface, lower C1–C4 together like the box.
    • Wick thin superglue into the four seams.

    CheckFrame lies flat with no rock.

  2. 10

    Fit the captive nuts

    • Drop an M4 nut into each of the 12 hex pockets on the back of the flange.
    • Lock each with a drop of superglue (keep it out of the thread).

    CheckA bolt from the front picks up every nut.

  3. 11

    Lay the gasket

    • Stick the 10 × 8 mm EPDM strip to the flange face with its outer edge on the guide line, starting and finishing at a bottom corner.
    • Butt the ends tight and dab silicone on the join.

    CheckStrip follows the guide line all round, not stretched at the corners.

  4. 12

    Mount the filter

    • Seat the filter on the gasket, fibrous pre-filter face towards the box.
    • Fit the clamp and run the 12 bolts in a criss-cross until the clamp touches the flange all round.

    CheckClamp touches the flange everywhere. That is the designed squeeze: 5.5 mm at the thin spots, 2.5 mm at the thick ones. Stop tightening.

Fan and intake

  1. 13

    Seat the fan

    • Lay the TPU ring on the socket ledge.
    • Lower the fan in, airflow arrow pointing down, gland towards the back.

    CheckFan sits level on the ring and doesn't rock.

  2. 14

    Fit the bell

    • Push the bell over the fan's intake end until the stop lip lands on the rim, gland through the notch.

    CheckA gentle pull doesn't lift it.

  3. 15

    Disc and lid

    • Lay the Ø224 mm floss disc on the grid (carbon sheet underneath if you use one).
    • Fit the lid: push down through the slots, twist clockwise to the stop.

    CheckLid won't lift. Disc edge clamped all round.

Visor

Detachable clear hood. It hangs by its rear lip on top of the clamp frame; the magnets just stop it getting knocked off.

  1. 16

    Join the visor halves

    • Canopy face down on the flat surface, lower V2 onto V1's three jigsaw knobs.
    • Wick thin superglue along the canopy seam, the strip and the front rib.

    CheckCanopy flat across the seam. No step at the front edge.

  2. 17

    Clamp magnets

    • Glue a 6 × 3 mm magnet into each of the 8 pockets on the clamp front, all the same pole facing out.
    • Test every one with a loose magnet before the glue goes off.

    CheckThe loose magnet pulls onto all 8 with the same face.

  3. 18

    Visor magnets, the foolproof way

    • Stick one of the remaining 8 magnets onto each clamp magnet (it self-orients), and put a tiny dab of superglue at the bottom of each visor pocket.
    • Hang the visor on by its lip, press it home for 60 s, then lift it off. The magnets come with it, the right way round.

    CheckVisor clicks on, bolt heads land in their pockets, and it pulls off cleanly.

7 · Test

The plastic is finished. Two quick tests. A lighter and an incense stick, no lab kit.

19

Flame test

  • With the visor on, hold a lighter flame 5–10 cm in front of the filter at each of the 9 points.
  • Turn the dial on the plug-in speed controller until the flame leans about 45° and settles.

CheckAbout 45° and dead steady at all 9 points. A flame that dances means turbulence, so adjust the speed until it settles. If it only dances near the edges, check the clamp seal. The usable zone ends roughly 19 cm (7.5 in) out from the filter.

20

Smoke and plates

  • Draw an incense stick slowly round the clamp edge, then across the face.
  • Run an open-plate test in the airstream.

CheckSmoke runs straight out with no curl-back at the edges, and no smoke escapes round the clamp. Plates stay clean. An aftermarket "H13" label is a claim, and the plates are the test of it.

Air spreader inside the box

Not fitted in v1. If the flame leans much harder on the bottom row than the top, add a deflector under the fan outlet. Otherwise leave it out.
